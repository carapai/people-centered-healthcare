# Research: patient matching and MPI for a national-scale, offline-first registry

Issue: #10. Spec context: `MASTER_PROMPT.md` §18 (`health-client-registry`), Part 9 (§57–62), §87–89. Researched 2026-10-27. Claims marked **unverified** could not be confirmed against a primary source in this session.

## Summary (answer to the question)

1. **Use layered matching, not one algorithm.** First, a deterministic pass on strong, verified identifiers: NIN when NIRA has verified it, the platform's own health ID, and program IDs such as ART numbers. Second, a **Fellegi–Sunter probabilistic score** on demographics (names, birth date, sex, phone, village/parish). The score uses term-frequency adjustment and **two thresholds**: above the upper one is an auto-link, the band between is a "potential match" for human review, and below the lower one is a new identity. This combines the OpenCR model (which ships both deterministic and probabilistic rule sets with `autoMatchThreshold`/`potentialMatchThreshold`) with Splink's documented Fellegi–Sunter practice. Blocking is required at national scale.
2. **Link, don't destroy.** Use an **OpenCR-style golden-record model**. Every source registration, including each provisional Edge registration, stays a `Patient` with its own id. Source records point to a central golden `Patient` via `Patient.link type=refer`, and the golden record points back with `seealso`. This keeps offline-created references valid and makes unlinking possible. True duplicates *within* one authority use FHIR `$merge` semantics (`replaced-by`/`replaces`, source set `active=false`), and only after human approval.
3. **Edge registrations are provisional by construction.** An Edge or desktop node mints a client UUID plus a node-scoped provisional identifier, and never mints a national/health ID offline. It may run local `$match` against its cached slice. On sync, central runs `$match`. Auto-links are pushed back down as link updates. Potential matches go to a review queue, and their clinical data stays attached to the provisional Patient in the meantime. DHIS2 and OpenSRP both rely on **pre-reserved ID pools** for offline creation. That approach is an option for a human-readable health ID, with known exhaustion and expiry problems.
4. **Model caregivers as `RelatedPerson` (role) plus `Person` (the human).** The caregiver's own `Patient`, if they have one, is linked through `Person.link`. A login account maps to a `Person`, and `Person.link` lists the Patients and RelatedPersons it may act as, with assurance `level1`–`level4`. This matches §58's identity separation exactly.
5. **NIN is a strong but incomplete key in Uganda.** NIRA reports about 75% of the population registered (Sept 2026). Birth registration of under-ones is low. A NIRA system-to-system verification interface exists through MoU, VPN and a Yes/No or with-consent full-detail response, and it is free for government. The NIN can therefore anchor identity, but it cannot be *required*. No source confirmed a live national health client registry or a MoH-issued health ID in production as of today (**unverified**).

## Cited findings

### Fellegi–Sunter and modern probabilistic linkage
- Origin: Fellegi, I.P. & Sunter, A.B. (1969), "A Theory for Record Linkage", *JASA* 64(328):1183–1210. https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1969.10501049 (bibliographic citation from prior knowledge; full text not fetched in this session).
- Splink's account of the model: the m probability measures data quality (agreement given a true match), and the u probability measures coincidence/cardinality (agreement given a non-match). Each comparison contributes a Bayes factor m/u. Match weight is log2 of that factor plus a prior from λ. Weights are additive under the independence assumption, and the total converts to a match probability that is then thresholded. https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/theory/fellegi_sunter.html
- **Term-frequency adjustment:** the basic model "doesn't account for skew in the distributions of linking variables". TF adjustment raises the weight for rare values ("Portia") and lowers it for common ones ("Jack"). It does not depend on m, so EM does not need to estimate it. `tf_minimum_u_value` caps the boost from rare values, which may be typos. This matters for Ugandan names, where some surnames and clan names are very common. https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/comparisons/term-frequency.html
- **Blocking:** the number of pairs is n(n-1)/2, so 1M records gives about 500 billion pairs. Blocking rules are "the single most important determinant of how quickly your linkage runs". A longer list of strict rules is better than a few loose ones. Blocking for prediction is a separate concern from blocking for training. https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/topic_guides/blocking/blocking_rules.html
- Splink trains m/u without labels (EM). The training guide was not fetched in this session (**unverified** here). Splink is Python/SQL (DuckDB/Spark), not Rust, so we would use it offline for calibration (see Implications).
- OpenHIE Patient Identity Management Toolkit: it describes deterministic rules (e.g. exact first and last name) versus probabilistic scores with a cut-off. It warns that "automated methods without thorough manual review increase the risk of inappropriately identifying separate individuals". It recommends ongoing monitoring in three phases: characterize data and choose an algorithm, match and adjudicate, then review performance. https://guides.ohie.org/duc/patient-identity-management-toolkit/modules/key-considerations-in-matching/background

### OpenHIE Client Registry spec
- Required workflows: create (CRWF-1), update (CRWF-2), query by identifier (CRWF-3), query by demographics (CRWF-4). Required functions: linking/de-duplication with a view of merge/link operations (CRF-2), an accept/reject UI for merge suggestions that allows field-level choice (CRF-2c), and full audit (CRF-8). Recommended functions: configurable match rules and blocking that non-programmers can adjust (CRF-1, CRF-6), a potential-match review UI (CRF-5), parent/child relationships and multiple-birth support (CRF-10), and syncing client IDs with the Shared Health Record (CRF-4). https://guides.ohie.org/arch-spec/openhie-component-specifications-1/client-registry

### OpenCR (OpenHIE reference client registry)
- Purpose and limits: it assigns and looks up unique IDs and accepts FHIR from point-of-service systems. It is "not a Shared Health Record", and it does not deduplicate records *inside* the point-of-service systems. https://intrahealth.github.io/client-registry/
- Architecture: a Node.js OpenCR service, a HAPI FHIR server, OpenSearch/Elasticsearch, an optional review UI ("view and break matches"), and optional OpenHIM. https://intrahealth.github.io/client-registry/admin/architecture/
- Decision rules use FHIRPath per field and the algorithms jaro-winkler, damerau-levenshtein and exact. Elasticsearch-side fuzzy matching supports only edit distance ≤ 2. https://intrahealth.github.io/client-registry/admin/decision_rules/
- Source (`intrahealth/client-registry`, last push 2025-04-01):
  - `server/config/decisionRules.json` shows `"matchingType": "deterministic"` over given/family (jaro-winkler 0.8/0.9), birthDate, phone, national ID and ART number (exact), with `"potentialMatchThreshold": 4`, `"autoMatchThreshold": 6` and a gender filter (blocking). https://github.com/intrahealth/client-registry/blob/master/server/config/decisionRules.json
  - `decisionRulesProbabilisticTemplate.json` shows `"matchingType": "probabilistic"` with per-field `mValue`/`uValue` (i.e. Fellegi–Sunter). It has explicit null handling: `conservative` (null is not a match), `moderate` (null does not affect the score) and `greedy` (null counts as a match). https://github.com/intrahealth/client-registry/blob/master/server/config/decisionRulesProbabilisticTemplate.json
  - `server/lib/mixins/matchMixin.js` `addLinks()`: the source patient gets `link {other: golden, type: 'refer'}` and the golden record gets `link {other: source, type: 'seealso'}`. A golden record is created when nothing matches. Human-adjudication tags preserve manual decisions against later auto-matching. https://github.com/intrahealth/client-registry/blob/master/server/lib/mixins/matchMixin.js
- Uganda: the OpenCR prototype was "informed by stakeholders in Uganda", namely MoH and the Central Public Health Laboratory (CPHL). https://www.measureevaluation.org/resources/publications/fs-20-443.html. Whether OpenCR is in national production use in Uganda today is **unverified**.

### FHIR R5: `$match`, `$merge`, Patient.link
- `Patient/$match` (Trial Use) has inputs `resource` (which "does not have to be complete, nor does it need to pass validation"), `onlyCertainMatches` and `count`. It returns a searchset Bundle ordered by likelihood, with `search.score` 0..1 and the `match-grade` extension (the example shows `certain`/`possible`; the full code list `certain|probable|possible|certain-nonmatch` is from prior knowledge, **unverified** here). No match gives an empty bundle with no error. The algorithm is implementation-defined. Inactive patients that have a merge target may be excluded. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/patient-operation-match.html
- `Patient/$merge` (Trial Use, maturity 0, not idempotent) takes `source-patient`, `target-patient` (by reference or identifiers that resolve to exactly one), `result-patient` (which "MUST have the link property included referencing the source") and `preview`. The source gets `link type=replaced-by` and is set to `active=false`. Deleting the source is outside the standard operation. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/patient-operation-merge.html
- Link-type codes: `replaced-by` means "must no longer be used". `replaces` means "is the current active patient record". `refer` means the record is "in use and valid but not considered the main source of information". `seealso` means it is "in use and valid" but points to another Patient/RelatedPerson. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/codesystem-link-type.html
- Patient page notes: about 2% of registrations are in error, mostly duplicates. Merging and linking are the two approaches. `refer` is used for Patient-index links and the target does not point back. `seealso` links need not be bilateral. Patient.contact is for contact details, cannot be a reference target, and RelatedPerson must be used when a reference is needed. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/patient.html
- IHE PMIR models a single "Patient Master Identity (a.k.a. Golden Patient record)" in a registry fed by ITI-93 (create/update/delete/merge) and ITI-94 subscriptions, reusing PDQm (ITI-78) and PIXm (ITI-83). "The subsumed patient identity is deprecated". Unmerge is out of scope. https://profiles.ihe.net/ITI/PMIR/volume-1.html

### FHIR R5: Person and RelatedPerson
- Person is a linkage resource. It "SHALL NOT be referenced by any other clinical or administrative resources". Actors are always Patient, Practitioner or RelatedPerson. `Person.link.assurance` uses levels `level1` to `level4`, based on NIST: probabilistic matching corresponds to `level1` and a government photo ID to `level3`. Person can back a portal user's access to "their data (via the Person.link Patient entries)". The page warns that the link itself can disclose sensitive information. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/person.html
- A RelatedPerson has a personal or non-healthcare relationship to the patient and is "primarily used for attribution". The page gives "A patient's attorney or guardian" as an example. The relationship code is bound with preferred strength to PatientRelationshipType. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/relatedperson.html

## Implications for the decision

### Matching approach
- Build a Rust matching engine inside `health-client-registry`, configured by versioned **decision-rule metadata** in the OpenCR shape: per-field FHIRPath, comparator (exact, Jaro-Winkler, Damerau-Levenshtein, phonetic), m/u values, null handling, blocking keys, and auto/potential thresholds. Publish it through the metadata layer (§41, §45) so countries can tune it without code changes, as CRF-1/CRF-6 require.
- Pipeline: (1) **deterministic short-circuit** on NIRA-verified NIN or an existing platform health ID. (2) **Blocking** on several strict keys, e.g. phonetic(family)+birth year, phone, NIN, and district+given-initial. (3) **Fellegi–Sunter scoring** with TF adjustment. (4) Classify into auto-link, potential (review queue) or new. An identifier *conflict*, such as two different verified NINs, is a hard non-match regardless of score.
- Calibrate m/u and thresholds **offline with Splink (EM)** on de-identified Ugandan data such as UgandaEMR extracts, then export the parameters into the rule metadata. Re-calibrate periodically, and track precision/recall from reviewer decisions.
- Expose matching as FHIR `Patient/$match` with `search.score` and `match-grade`. The same engine (smaller corpus) runs on the Edge against its cached facility slice for "did you mean" prompts at registration.
- Candidate generation needs indexes for blocking keys and phonetic codes in SurrealDB. Full-text or fuzzy capability needs to be validated against SurrealDB (an open question).

### Provisional registration reconciliation
- Edge/desktop creates a `Patient` with a client-generated UUID, an identifier in a node-scoped system (e.g. `urn:…/provisional/{node-id}`), and `meta.tag = provisional`. National or health IDs are never minted offline. If a human-readable facility number is needed, use DHIS2/OpenSRP-style reserved pools and plan for exhaustion.
- On sync, central runs the matcher (§87: "Provisional creation allowed → identity matching and reconciliation"):
  - Auto-link: the provisional Patient gets `link refer → golden` and the tag changes to `reconciled`. The link change replicates *down* to the Edge, and clinical resources keep referencing the local Patient id.
  - Potential: a review Task opens (a staff UI per CRF-5), and the Patient stays provisional and fully usable for care.
  - New: a golden record is created and a health ID issued, then replicated down.
- Demographic conflicts follow §89 (review, never LWW). Reviewer decisions are stored as human-adjudication markers, as OpenCR does, so later auto-runs do not undo them.
- Self-registration (§59) runs the same pipeline. Its "Matching → Staff review" step is the potential-match queue, and phone-based accounts are never auto-linked (§58).

### Merge / link model
- **Default is linking.** Source (facility or provisional) Patients link `refer` to the golden Patient, and the golden record links `seealso` back. This is reversible: unlinking is just editing links, plus audit (`Provenance`/`AuditEvent`).
- **Merge** is used only for confirmed same-authority duplicates. It follows `$merge` semantics: target gets `replaces`, source gets `replaced-by` and `active=false`, there is a `preview` step, and human approval is required. Reference rewriting of clinical resources is a separate, explicit, audited job, or it can be left to `replaced-by` resolution at read time. PMIR treats unmerge as administrative, so we must design our own unmerge.
- The Care Journey (§61) and search resolve through the golden record so that linked records appear as one patient.

### Caregiver modelling
- Login account → `Person`, outside clinical data. `Person.link` → own `Patient` (assurance by verification: `level1` from self-asserted or probabilistic matching, `level3` after NIN/photo-ID check) and → `RelatedPerson` resources for each dependent.
- `RelatedPerson(patient=child, relationship=MTH/GUARD…)` is the actor in Consent, QuestionnaireResponse.author, Communication and similar resources. Access requires a `Consent` grant plus verification. A mother's own `Patient` and her `RelatedPerson`-for-child are linked through `Person`, not through Patient.link. Mother–newborn and multiple-birth links also cover OpenHIE CRF-10.

### Risks
- False merges or links are a patient-safety risk (OpenHIE toolkit warning). Keep auto-link thresholds conservative at first, and make every link reversible.
- About 25% of people lack a NIN and most infants are unregistered. Matching must work well without a NIN.
- NIRA access requires MoU, VPN and onboarding. Edges cannot verify NIN offline, so NIN verification happens centrally and asynchronously.
- Name variability (clan names, order swaps, spelling) and shared phones (one phone across many family members) reduce discriminating power. Phone needs a low weight and TF adjustment.
- Splink calibration needs a representative dataset, which may be hard to obtain under the 2019 Data Protection and Privacy Act.
- `$match` and `$merge` are Trial Use, and `$merge` is maturity 0, so the semantics may change.
- Person links can leak sensitive associations (FHIR Person security note). Link visibility must follow confidentiality (§96).
